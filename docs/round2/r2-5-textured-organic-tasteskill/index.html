The board through the day. A briefing in the morning.

The board holds what is coming and stays out of the way. The briefing does something else with the same entries: it decides what actually counts today, and it names what has been sitting untouched for a while.

That second part is where it stops being a calendar. A calendar can only show you what you already wrote down at the right time.

The briefing arrives as an email, at the hour you set, and not in the chat. If you run the whole thing yourself, that means a mail server in the configuration.

One message in, and that is the whole board. It shows what is there rather than filling the panel with something.

It runs on a screen you already have.

The board is a greyscale PNG, fetched over HTTP. Anything that can pull an image and keep it on the glass is a surface for it. In the order they cost you money:

  1. Free

    The phone in your pocket

    Open the board's address, add it to the home screen. It is the same picture the wall would be showing, and it is the fastest way to find out whether you want a wall at all.

  2. Free, if it still charges

    An old tablet

    Kiosk mode, brightness down, propped on the shelf you walk past. The one in the drawer counts. It will glow, and it will want a cable.

  3. Free, it is already on

    A browser tab on the box

    If a machine in the house is running anyway, the board can live in a tab on the monitor beside it. No second device, no mount, no drilling.

  4. From about €120

    A TRMNL

    It speaks BYOS out of the box, so you point it at your own endpoint and it stops talking to anyone else. Bought from them, not from me.

  5. Whatever the panel cost you

    Any bare E-Ink panel

    If you already drive a panel from a Pi or an ESP32, this is one more image URL to poll. BYOS is an open protocol and no TRMNL account is involved.

E-Ink is the one it was built for.

  • No backlight, so a dark kitchen stays dark and the board reads like something printed rather than something on.
  • No microphone and no camera in the room. The panel only ever asks for a picture, and nothing on it ever listens.
  • Cut the power and the last board stays on the glass, which is a property no lit screen in the house has.

That is the best surface, not the requirement. Everything above it on the list is a real way to run this, and the free one at the top is the sensible place to start.

Three ways to run it.

What changes between them is who does the work, who is liable, and who notices when it breaks.

All on your machine

One compose file brings up Postgres, Whisper, Ollama and the application. It wants a real box: a NUC, an old desktop, something with about 8 GB of memory to spare, because the embedding model alone is 4.7 GB on disk. A Raspberry Pi will carry the application quite happily. It will not carry the transcription.

Your machine, heavy parts on APIs you pick

The same install, the same compose file. Transcription and the language model go out to a provider you choose, on your key and your account, so the terms you are living under are the ones you agreed to. Your contract, not mine. This is the version that fits on a Pi.

My machine

No install, no server, no updates, nothing to keep awake at three in the morning. You tap an invite link, the bot answers, and the panel on your wall starts fetching a board.

What you would be paying for in the third one is not hosting. I transcribe, I sort, and I notice when it breaks. Those are precisely the three parts that weak hardware and an unattended cupboard cannot do.

“So I need an API subscription as well?”

For the sorting, barely. It is a small job for a small model. A household sends something like fifty messages a month; each is a few hundred tokens in and a few hundred back, call it a thousand for the round trip, plus one digest a day. That is under a hundred thousand tokens a month, and small models are priced somewhere around thirty cents per million.

Three to five cents a month for the sorting, on somebody else's model. Transcription is the part that costs real money when you rent it by the minute, and that is the argument for keeping Whisper at home.

What actually leaves your house

Not “nothing”, in any of the three. A voice message reaches Telegram's servers and is downloaded back from them before anything of yours or mine has touched it. Here is the whole of it, per mode.

What leaves All on your machine Your machine, your APIs My machine
The audio Telegram, then your own box Telegram, then the provider you picked Telegram, then mine
The text of the entry Stays on your box Your language model provider sees it Mine, and nowhere onward from there
The board image Your box to your panel Your box to your panel My box to your panel, over the internet
The weather open-meteo is called with your coordinates on every single render, in all three modes. There is no offline board.
Your calendar Google, and only if you connect one. Leave it unconnected and nothing is asked of them.

Local transcription is still worth having, and this is the honest reason why: the audio is not handed to a second company, and the words you said go only where you pointed them. That is a smaller claim than the one usually made for it, and it is the one that holds.

Two doors, for two different people.

Nothing is gated behind the paid one. No licence check, no reduced feature set, no code path that behaves differently depending on who started the container. It is the same AGPL-3.0 application through either door. What differs is what each one hands you, and each hands you something the other cannot.

Nothing of yours reaches me at all.

Not the audio, not the text, not a count of how often you use it. There is nothing to trust me with, because I am not standing in the path.

And one person running a box for their own household sits inside the household exemption of the GDPR, Article 2(2)(c). Nobody in that kitchen is anybody's data controller. No processing agreement, no register, no deletion procedure, no me. That is a legal position I cannot sell you at any price.

You give a box, the power it draws, and the attention to notice when it stops.

I run it

It runs without you.

Updates, a model that changes underneath you, a disk quietly filling: my problem, on my morning, before you are awake.

And the half that is less flattering to the other door: a self-hosted setup fails silently. The bot stops answering, the panel shows Tuesday for three days, and you do not notice, because this is exactly the thing you never open. Run by me, somebody notices. That is most of what you are buying.

You give money.

What I do with what reaches me

  • Encrypted on the way in and encrypted on the disk it lands on.
  • Never used to train a model, never sold, never passed to an advertiser.
  • The recording is thrown away once it has been transcribed. The text is what is kept.
  • The subprocessors are named rather than described: Telegram carries the message in, open-meteo answers the weather call, Google only if you connect a calendar, and the company whose machine it runs on is named in the terms.
  • One request deletes the household and everything in it, and it is a button, not a support ticket.

Voice at the wall is not new.

Echo Show has done it for years, at €329, and does it well. Saying otherwise would be the fastest way to lose this audience.

The difference here is narrow and worth stating exactly: a free-form spoken thought rather than a list command, something that structures it instead of filing the sentence verbatim, running on a machine you control, ending on E-Ink that sits in the room without glowing at it.

What it does

  • Takes a message in Telegram, typed or spoken.
  • Transcribes the recording, then throws the recording away.
  • Turns the sentence into entries and files them by itself.
  • Renders the board and serves it to the panel over BYOS.
  • Sends the morning briefing by email at the hour you set.

What it gets wrong

  • It puts a Thursday appointment on Tuesday, because “day after tomorrow” was said on a Monday evening about a Wednesday.
  • It splits one thought into two entries, or folds two into one.
  • It hears the wrong name in a kitchen with the extractor fan on.
  • What it does not do is lose the thing. It is on the board, in the wrong place, on the one screen in the house you actually look at. You say so in the same chat you spoke into, and the board redraws.

How often it gets that wrong is structurally unmeasurable.

Not unmeasured, unmeasurable: there is no ground truth to score it against. Whether “half three, day after tomorrow, and don't forget the bins” was read correctly depends on the household that said it, what that household usually means by it, and which of the two things it actually cared about. A test set for that is its own project, and it would be a test set of one family's habits. So there is no accuracy figure on this page, and anyone quoting one for a task shaped like this is quoting a number they chose.

For anyone who wants to look.

Pipeline

Telegram bot
Text or voice in, one channel, nothing to open. A voice file is downloaded back from Telegram's servers before it is transcribed.
Transcription
Whisper on your machine, or an API call if the box cannot carry it.
Structuring
A language model, Ollama locally or an API, the same choice again.
Embeddings
qwen3-embedding at 4096 dimensions, about 4.7 GB on disk. This is what sets the 8 GB floor.
Render
Satori to resvg, TypeScript, out as a 4-bit greyscale PNG.
Delivery
The open BYOS protocol. The panel polls, the server never pushes.

Also in there

Weather
open-meteo, called on every render in every mode. The board is never fully offline.
Morning digest
Written by the model, delivered over SMTP. Self-hosting it means a mail server in the configuration.
Calendar
Google, if you connect one. Nothing is asked of them if you do not.
MCP interface
So other tools can reach the same entries. Not a headline, on purpose.
Semantic search
Present, and equally not the point. If searching is what you want, this is the wrong project.
