Where it runs.

Three ways. What changes is who does the work, who is liable, and who notices when it breaks.

All on your machine

Compose brings up Postgres, Whisper, Ollama and the app. It wants a real box: a NUC, an old desktop, roughly 8 GB of RAM free, most of it for the models.

A Raspberry Pi runs the application quite happily. It does not run the transcription.

The morning digest goes out over SMTP, so a mail server is yours to point it at.

Your machine, heavy parts on APIs you pick

The same install. Transcription and the language model live at a provider you chose, under a key you pasted in, on terms you agreed to.

Your contract, not mine.

Nothing heavy runs locally, so this one fits on a Pi.

My machine

No install, no server, no updates, no version you are three behind. You tap an invite link and start talking to it.

Everything below happens on hardware I am responsible for, which is the part that is actually being sold.

What you buy is not hosting. I transcribe, I sort, and I notice when it breaks.

Those are precisely the three things a small box cannot do for you: the models are too big for it, and nobody is watching it.

What actually leaves your house.

Every mode sends something out, including the one that runs entirely on your own hardware, because the voice message reaches Telegram's servers before anything of mine touches it. Here is the whole of it, drawn per mode.

Local transcription is still worth having. Telegram carries the audio either way, and that is a fact about the input channel, not a thing any mode can undo. What local transcription buys you is that the recording is never handed to a second company, and that the text of what you said goes only where you point it.

Two doors, and they are for different people. Not a free tier and a paid tier: the same software, running in a different room.

You run it

  • Nothing of yours reaches me at all. Not the audio, not the text, not the board. There is no account on my side for it to reach.
  • The household exemption. One person running a box for their own household sits inside the GDPR's personal and household exemption, Art. 2(2)(c). Nobody is anybody's data controller, and that includes me.
  • What it asks of you. A box, the power it draws, and the attention to notice when it stops.
  • “So I need an API subscription too?” No. Sixty messages in a month, each with a schema and a couple of examples in front of it, is about 90,000 tokens in and 12,000 out. Thirty morning digests add roughly 120,000 in and 12,000 out. At what small models charge per million tokens, that lands between three and five cents a month. It is not a subscription, it is a rounding error.

I run it

  • It runs without you. No install, no server, no updates, no migration you have to read about first.
  • Somebody notices. A self-hosted setup fails silently. The bot stops, the panel shows Tuesday for three days, and you never find out, because this is exactly the thing you never open. Run by me, someone is watching that.
  • What it asks of you. Money, and the fact that your entries sit on a machine you do not own.
  • How they are held. Encrypted in transit and at rest. No training on your entries, no resale, subprocessors named in the terms, everything deletable on request, and the recording is discarded as soon as it has been transcribed.

One config field, not a different product. There is no locked tier, no licence check, and nothing in the code that behaves differently depending on who is running it. It is the same source either way, and the source is public.

Honest status.

Not a first

Echo Show has done voice at the wall for years, at €329, and it does it well.

The difference here is narrow and I would rather state it small and true than large and vague: a free-form spoken sentence instead of a list command, an actual structuring step behind it that decides what is a dated thing and what is not, processing you can keep on your own machine, and E-Ink instead of a lit screen in the kitchen. That is the whole of it. Anyone telling you nobody else does voice at the wall is selling you something.

What it does
  • Telegram in, text and voice
  • Transcription, on your machine or at a provider you pick
  • Structuring into dated events and undated reminders
  • A greyscale render, fetched over HTTP
  • Delivery over BYOS to a real panel

Not measured

Accuracy of the structuring step is structurally unmeasurable.

Not “we are getting around to it”. There is no labelled set, and there is no agreed ground truth for what the correct output even is when someone mumbles a sentence at their phone with two things in it and half a date. I have no benchmark to point you at, so I am not going to quote you a percentage. If that number is the thing you would need before you would run this, put that in the box above and say so plainly.

When it gets it wrong

It will put something in the wrong column, and you should know what that looks like before you decide.

A thing that should have carried a date arrives under Don't Forget with no date on it. Half three gets read as next Thursday instead of this one. A single sentence with two thoughts in it comes out as one entry rather than two. That is the shape of the failure.

What does not happen: nothing is deleted, nothing is silently dropped, and the message you sent is still sitting in the chat exactly as you sent it. You fix it by saying so, in the same place you said the first thing. The failure mode is a wrong column, not a lost thought, and that is the whole reason the structuring step is allowed to be imperfect.

Technical.

Input
Telegram bot. Text or voice message. The audio passes through Telegram's servers before anything else touches it.
Transcription
Whisper on your own machine, or a provider you pick and pay directly.
Structuring
A language model, local or via API. Output is typed records, not free text.
Search
qwen3-embedding, 4096 dimensions. The model is about 4.7 GB on disk, which is what sets the RAM floor.
Hardware
Roughly 8 GB of RAM free for the all-local mode. A Raspberry Pi runs the app but not the transcription.
Render
Satori to resvg. A greyscale PNG, fetched over HTTP.
Weather
open-meteo, called on every render, in every mode.
Delivery
Open BYOS protocol over HTTP. No TRMNL account.
Digest
One email each morning, over SMTP. Top 3 today, stuck on, small win. Self-hosted, the mail server is yours.
Interfaces
An MCP server and semantic search across your own entries. Both exist. Neither is the point of the product.
