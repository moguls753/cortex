Three ways to run it.

The software is the same in all three. What changes is who does the work, who is liable, and who notices when it breaks. Under each one is the honest list of what leaves your house in that mode.

01

All on your machine

One compose file brings up Postgres, Whisper, Ollama and the app. It wants a real box — a NUC, an old desktop, something with about 8 GB free. A Raspberry Pi will run the app, but not the transcription.

02

Your machine, heavy parts on APIs you pick

The same install, with transcription and the language model pointed at a provider you choose, on a key that is yours. Your contract, not mine. This one fits on a Pi.

03

My machine

No install, no server, no updates, nothing to keep an eye on. You tap an invite link, and the bot is already awake. This is the one that is for sale.

“Nothing leaves your house” is not a sentence I can write.

A Telegram bot works by downloading the voice file back off Telegram's servers, so the recording is there before anything of yours or mine has touched it. The weather calls open-meteo on every render, in every mode. The morning digest goes out over SMTP, so a mail server sees it. A connected calendar means Google. Those three drawings are the whole list.

Local transcription is still worth having, for a narrower reason than the usual one: the audio is not handed to a second company, and the text only ever goes where you point it. That is a smaller promise than other pages make. It has the advantage of being true.

And the objection mode 02 always gets — “so now I need an API subscription too”. Not really. A household puts in maybe five things a day; call it 150 sentences a month, roughly 1,200 tokens in and 150 out for each one. At small-model rates that is about €0.03–0.05 a month. Transcribing a seven-second clip is a rounding error next to it, and nothing at all if you keep Whisper at home.

What is true, and what I cannot tell you.

Voice at the wall is not new. An Echo Show has done it for years, for €329, and it does it well.

What is different here is narrower than a category, so here it is exactly: you speak a free-form thought rather than a list command; something actually structures it into fields instead of appending a string; you choose whose hardware it runs on, including your own; and the output is E-Ink, a surface that is simply there on a wall, not a screen you have to wake. That is the whole claim. If a smart display already covers what you need, it probably does.

What it does
Telegram bot, text and voice. Transcription. Structuring by a language model. The board rendered server-side to a PNG (Satori → resvg, 4-bit greyscale) and served over BYOS. The morning email.
Not measured
How often the sorting gets it right is unmeasurable, and not in the "we haven't got round to it" sense. There is no test set, no ground truth and no benchmark for "did it file this sentence the way a person would have". So there is no accuracy figure on this page, and anyone quoting you one for this kind of system, me included, would be making it up.
When it is wrong
It files something under the wrong heading, or misses a date and leaves the reminder without one. Nothing is lost when that happens: the entry is still there, the original message is still in the chat, and you say what it should have been and it changes. It is wrong the way a person is wrong, not the way a form is wrong.
What it asks
Self-hosting means a compose file, a terminal, a Telegram bot token of your own, and — if you want the morning email — an SMTP server you point it at. None of that is hard. All of it is an evening. The hosted mode is where that evening goes away.
One household
One install serves one household. There are no separate accounts inside it and no per-person view, which is deliberate: the household is the unit here, and it is why the price is per household rather than per head.

Input
A Telegram bot, text or voice. The voice file is fetched back off Telegram's own servers before anything else happens to it — that is how the Bot API works, and it is true in all three modes. Whichever box you chose then transcribes it, with Whisper.
Structuring
A language model, local or behind an API, whichever your hardware and your taste allow.
Board
Rendered server-side to a PNG through Satori → resvg, 4-bit greyscale, served over the open BYOS protocol. The device pulls; nothing is pushed at it, and there is no interaction on the panel itself.
Morning email
A second pass over the same store, delivered by SMTP at a time you set — an email, not a chat message. Self-hosted, that means giving it a mail server.
Calendar
Optional. If you connect one it is the Google Calendar API over OAuth, against googleapis.com/calendar/v3, and the board's Week Ahead is read from it. Leave it unconnected and no request is ever made.
Weather
open-meteo, called on every render, in every mode. Coordinates only; no key, no account, no other endpoint involved.
Also in there
An MCP server over the same store, and semantic search across what you have said, on a local embedding model that is about 4.7 GB on disk. Neither is the point of the product, which is why they are down here and not at the top.
What it wants
Mode 01 wants roughly 8 GB of RAM free: Whisper, a language model and that embedding model, with Postgres underneath. Mode 02 fits on a Raspberry Pi, because the two heavy things have left the box. Mode 03 wants a phone.
Deployment
Your machine, or mine. One configuration field, not a fork, and no licence check anywhere in it.
