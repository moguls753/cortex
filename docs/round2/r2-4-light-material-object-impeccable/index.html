Where it runs. Three ways.

The same software in all three — same repository, same containers, same board. What changes is who does the work, who is liable, and who notices when it breaks.

All on your machine

docker compose up -d brings up Postgres, a Whisper container, Ollama and the app. It wants a real box — a NUC, an old desktop, about 8 GB free. A Raspberry Pi runs the application but not the transcription.

Your machine, the heavy parts on APIs you pick

The same install, with transcription and the structuring model at a provider you chose, on a key you hold. Your contract, not mine. This one fits on a Pi.

My machine

No install, no server, no updates, no mail server to configure. Tap an invite link, point the bot at your household, and speak.

What you would be paying me for is not hosting. Hosting is a rented computer, and you can rent one cheaper than this. It is the three things a spare evening and a small board do not give you: I transcribe, I sort, and I notice when it breaks.

“Nothing leaves your house” would be untrue in all three, so it is not written anywhere on this page. If I run it, in plain terms

Encrypted in transit and at rest. Nothing you write is used to train a model, and nothing is sold or passed to an advertiser. Every company that touches your data is named on the privacy page, not described in the abstract. The recording is thrown away once it has been turned into text.

You can export the whole household as a file whenever you like, and deleting the account deletes the entries with it.

Two doors, and nothing behind them differs.

“So I need an API subscription as well?”

Not in any amount you will feel. A household puts perhaps 300 entries a month through the structuring step. At roughly 700 tokens in and 150 out per entry that is about 250,000 tokens a month, and on a small hosted model — a mini or a flash tier, at cents per million tokens — the whole month comes to three to five cents. Not euros.

If you already have a GPU it is zero. Transcription is the part that actually wants hardware, and that is the part you would either give 8 GB of RAM to or hand to a provider.

What works, what does not, and what nobody has measured.

What it does

  • Telegram capture, text and voice. The recording goes through Telegram and then to the machine you picked, and no further.
  • The structuring step, against a model of your choosing — a local one or a hosted API.
  • The wall render: a PNG produced server-side with Satori and resvg, 4-bit greyscale, pulled by the panel on a schedule.
  • The morning briefing, sent as an email over SMTP.
  • Postgres with pgvector, semantic search, and an MCP server with seven tools so an assistant can read and write the same entries.

What it does not do

  • There is no app. Capture is a Telegram bot and the output is an image on a wall. If Telegram is not something you are willing to use, none of this reaches you.
  • Fully local wants about 8 GB free. Whisper is roughly 3 GB and the embedding model about 4.7 GB. A Raspberry Pi runs the application and not the transcription.
  • Self-hosting the briefing means a mail server. The digest leaves over SMTP, so a self-hosted install needs working mail credentials.
  • One household uses it every day: mine. No testimonials, no case studies, no usage numbers, because there are none to have.
  • The repository is still called cortex. The name changed on 11 August 2026 and the container names and old links have not caught up.

Accuracy is unmeasurable right now, and I mean structurally.

Whether a spoken sentence was structured correctly has no answer that is not simply my opinion afterwards. Getting one needs a labelled set — real sentences with the intended output written down beside them — and the only person who could write that set today is the same person who wrote the thing being tested. That is not a measurement, it is a mirror.

So there is no accuracy figure anywhere on this page, and there will not be one until such a set exists and somebody other than me has checked it. If a wrong reading of your sentence would be a dealbreaker, that is a reasonable position and I have nothing to answer it with yet. That is exactly why the question near the top is the question, and why it is phrased as a trade rather than as a feature.

What I can tell you is what a wrong one looks like. Nothing is ever thrown away: a sentence it could not place lands as plain text rather than disappearing, and a dentist appointment filed as a reminder sits under the wrong heading until somebody moves it, which takes a moment on the dashboard. The failure is quiet, and the board is the last place you would look for it — that is the honest shape of the risk, and it is the same risk whoever runs the machine.
